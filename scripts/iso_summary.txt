ISOMORPHISM ANALYSIS FOR PLURIBUS-JUNIOR

Method: For each flop texture, compute how many turn+river outcomes collapse
to canonical representatives via suit isomorphism. Uses uniform ranges
(best case for iso — non-uniform ranges reduce the benefit).

Board Texture Distribution & Reduction:
  Rainbow (55%):     0.98x ratio (2% fewer outcomes)
  Two-tone (22%):    0.60x ratio (40% fewer)
  Monotone (5%):     0.29x ratio (71% fewer)
  Paired-rainbow (12%): 0.62x ratio (38% fewer)
  Paired-two-tone (5%): 0.60x ratio (40% fewer)
  Trips (1%):        0.31x ratio (69% fewer)

Weighted average: 0.79x ratio (21% fewer outcomes)
  → 2352 outcomes → ~1865 average canonical

Performance projection:
  Current: 2293ms/i
  With iso (avg): 2293 × 0.79 ≈ 1811ms/i
  With iso (best): 2293 × 0.29 = 665ms/i (monotone only)
  With iso (worst): 2293 × 0.98 = 2247ms/i (rainbow, 55% of flops)

The "roughly 6x" estimate was optimistic. Isomorphism provides:
- Massive benefit on rare textures (monotone, trips): 3-3.5x
- Moderate benefit on textured boards (paired, two-tone): 1.6-1.7x
- Negligible benefit on rainbow boards (majority): 1.02x
- Weighted average: 1.27x

The rainbow-board problem is fundamental: with 3 different suits on the
flop, no two suits have the same rankset, so no turn isomorphism exists.
River isomorphism only helps when adding the 4th card makes two suits'
ranksets match, which is uncommon (~2% reduction).

Implementation priority: Isomorphism is still worth doing (1.27x average),
but it's not the silver bullet. The convergence problem (15.4% exploitability
at 50 iters, target 3-5%) must be solved independently via streaming reduction
(Phase 2).

The streaming reduction addresses the ATOMIC APPROXIMATION in the batched
kernel, which is the root cause of slow convergence. Without fixing this,
even a free 6x speedup would leave the solver at ~10 iters in budget with
111% exploitability — still not viable.
